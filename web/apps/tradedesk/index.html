Trade Help

Value is driven primarily by each player's overall consensus rank (FantasyPros ECR, kept current via periodic Rest-of-Season refreshes), converted through a decay curve so being a WR7 overall means meaningfully more than being an RB7 overall by rank alone — a modest position-scarcity multiplier (RB and TE run slightly hotter, since they're scarcer at the replacement level) and a starter-tier premium then nudge value up for players who'd actually crack a typical 10-team starting lineup, and down for pure bench depth, since bench production doesn't score points. On top of that, four real per-player signals from FantasyPros nudge value further: a small early-season adjustment (±5%, skill positions only) for a clearly favorable or unfavorable Weeks 1–4 slate — the idea being a hot, easy-schedule start is a sell-high window, not a reason to overhaul a valuation — a full-season strength-of-schedule rating (±4%, every position, 1–5 stars), a fantasy-playoff-weeks schedule rating (±3%, 1–5 stars, since a brutal Weeks 15–17 slate matters even if the rest of the year looks fine), and an upside/bust spread (±6%) reflecting how much boom-or-bust risk is priced into a player's range of outcomes. None of these are large enough to flip a bad player into a good one — they shift things at the margins, the way a real analyst's notes would. On top of all of that, any player can be manually adjusted (the ± button in Rosters/Free Agents) for fast-breaking news — an injury, a role change — between full data refreshes. Starting-lineup impact assumes a 1 QB · 2 RB · 2 WR · 1 TE · 2 FLEX · 1 K · 1 DST lineup (5 bench, 3 IR) and compares each team's best possible starters before and after the deal. Chain paths are sequential, independent trades — the first happens on its own merits between two other teams, the next is your follow-up once it's actually happened — not a single combined transaction. Roster edits and completed trades are saved in this browser, and synced across your devices once a backend is connected under Settings › Cross-Device Sync.
